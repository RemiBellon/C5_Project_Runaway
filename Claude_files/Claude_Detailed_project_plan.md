# Runaway-Electron Orbit-Following on a Laptop: A Complete Plan to a Publishable Paper

## TL;DR
- **Commit to Angle (C): the conservation/structure properties of operator-split integrators once a dissipative synchrotron radiation-reaction (and collisional-drag) force is switched on.** This is the least-occupied niche: the fair, systematic integrator benchmark (Angle B) is essentially done by Ripperda et al. (ApJS 235, 21, 2018) for astrophysics, and the guiding-center-vs-full-orbit validity question (Angle A) is largely occupied by Liu/Wang/Qin (2016), Carbajal et al. (2017) and C. Liu et al. (2018). No one has published a clean study of *which* structure (phase-space volume is the wrong invariant once radiation reaction is on; the right diagnostic is the physically-known contraction rate of Hazeltine & Mahajan) an operator-split Boris/Vay/Higuera–Cary + Landau–Lifshitz scheme actually preserves or violates.
- **The current script's results are individually correct but collectively prove too little.** The 4.9×10⁻⁴ Larmor error and 3×10⁻³ μ conservation are consistent with second-order accuracy and adiabatic theory respectively, but there is no convergence study, the "bounded energy" claim is unsupported, the RK4 comparison is unfair (equal Δt, not equal cost), and the whole thing is non-relativistic — so it validates nothing that is publishable about *runaway* electrons yet.
- **Realistic path to a short, rigorous paper: ~6–10 months of part-time laptop work** (my estimate for one researcher in Python+NumPy+Numba with no HPC, not a published figure), staged verification-first, targeting either *Journal of Plasma Physics* / *Physics of Plasmas* (if the dissipative-structure result is genuinely new physics-numerics) or *Computer Physics Communications* / *European Journal of Physics* (if the contribution is the tested, archived code). A well-executed methodological or "negative" result IS publishable here.

## Key Findings

### Novelty verdict, blunt
**Angle B (integrator benchmark) is mostly closed.** Ripperda, Bacchini, Teunissen et al., *A Comprehensive Comparison of Relativistic Particle Integrators*, ApJS 235, 21 (2018), DOI 10.3847/1538-4365/aab114, already compares Boris, Vay (2008), Higuera–Cary (2017), a fully-implicit energy-conserving scheme and a relativistic guiding-center method, testing conservation of invariants and drift accuracy across non-, mildly-, and highly-relativistic regimes. Yasir & Saxena (arXiv:2410.03352, 2024) added an explicit equal-cost run-time comparison (reporting, e.g., ~91 operations/step for the Vay pusher). Redoing this in a circular-tokamak field with p_φ and μ diagnostics would be a *specific extension*, not a new result — publishable only as a pedagogical note at best.

**Angle A (GC vs full-orbit validity for REs) is largely occupied but has one genuine open corner.** The core physics — "collisionless / neoclassical pitch-angle scattering" and the breakdown of the lowest-order magnetic moment — was established by Liu, Wang & Qin, *Collisionless pitch-angle scattering of runaway electrons*, Nucl. Fusion 56, 064002 (2016), DOI 10.1088/0029-5515/56/6/064002; the corrected second-order magnetic moment was derived by C. Liu, Qin, Hirvijoki, Wang & Liu, *Conservative magnetic moment of runaway electrons and collisionless pitch-angle scattering*, Nucl. Fusion 58, 106018 (2018), DOI 10.1088/1741-4326/aad2a5 (via the Lie-perturbation method in the limit p∥ ≫ p⊥); full-orbit synchrotron consequences were mapped by Carbajal, del-Castillo-Negrete, Spong, Seal & Baylor, Phys. Plasmas 24, 042512 (2017), DOI 10.1063/1.4981209 (the KORC paper); and the high-order GC + synchrotron verification was done by Shi-Jie Liu, Wang, Liu, Hu, Wu, Liu & Wang, *Calculation of collisionless pitch-angle scattering of runaway electrons with synchrotron radiation via high-order guiding-centre equation*, J. Plasma Phys. 88, 5 (2022) (the "HGSA" approach). **However**, my dedicated search found no published *systematic multi-parameter map* of GC-breakdown thresholds spanning energy × pitch angle × aspect ratio × q-profile. Existing work fixes geometry to one or two machines (DIII-D-like, ITER-like) and scans only B₀/E/q or energy: Liu/Wang/Qin (2016) scan loop-E, B₀ and safety factor at fixed geometry; Carbajal et al. (2017) use DIII-D-like (R₀=1.5 m, a=0.5 m) and ITER-like parameters with q_edge = 2, 3 and energies to ~70 MeV. A systematic aspect-ratio × q sweep is genuinely open — but it is a large computational undertaking and risky on a laptop.

**Angle C (structure preservation with a dissipative force) is the most open and the best fit for a laptop.** The building blocks are all published separately — the Landau–Lifshitz (LL) form and its leap-frog implementation (Tamburini, Pegoraro, Di Piazza, Keitel & Macchi, New J. Phys. 12, 123005, 2010, DOI 10.1088/1367-2630/12/12/123005, which explicitly provides "a novel leap-frog pusher for its inclusion in particle-in-cell simulations"); the proof that radiation reaction physically *contracts* phase-space volume (Hazeltine & Mahajan, Phys. Rev. E 70, 046407, 2004; Lehmann & Spatschek, Phys. Rev. E 85, 056412, 2012); the fact that Boris/volume-preserving schemes owe their long-term fidelity to volume preservation (Qin, Zhang, Xiao, Liu, Sun & Tang, *Why is Boris algorithm so good?*, Phys. Plasmas 20, 084503, 2013; He, Sun, Liu & Qin, J. Comput. Phys. 281, 135, 2015); and the metriplectic / GENERIC structure-preserving program for dissipative kinetics (Morrison, *Structure and structure-preserving algorithms for plasma physics*, Phys. Plasmas 24, 055502, 2017; Kraus & Hirvijoki, Phys. Plasmas 24, 2017; Hirvijoki, Kraus & Burby, arXiv:1802.05263). **What is missing is the synthesis:** a careful demonstration of *what* an operator-split Boris/Vay/HC + LL scheme conserves and violates once dissipation is on, with the correct diagnostic (measured phase-space contraction rate vs the analytic Hazeltine–Mahajan rate) rather than the now-meaningless "volume preservation" test. That is a genuinely new, self-contained, laptop-scale contribution.

## Details

### 1. Recommended research question (commit to this)
> **"For relativistic runaway-electron orbits with synchrotron radiation reaction, which conservation/structure properties do the standard operator-split pushers (Boris, relativistic Boris, Vay, Higuera–Cary) actually preserve, and how should their long-time fidelity be diagnosed once the volume-preserving structure is deliberately broken by a physical dissipative force?"**

Reasoning: (i) it is defensible on a laptop because it lives in *short* physical windows and idealized fields where the answer is dominated by scheme structure, not by machine specifics; (ii) it has a clean, falsifiable core — the measured phase-space contraction rate should match the analytic Hazeltine–Mahajan rate for a correct scheme and deviate in a characterizable way for an inconsistent one; (iii) it is not occupied; (iv) it connects to a live methodological frontier (metriplectic integrators) so referees will see relevance. A secondary, lower-risk deliverable (a fair equal-cost Boris/Vay/HC benchmark in circular-tokamak geometry with p_φ, μ, energy diagnostics) can be bundled as validation and as the "conservative" fallback if the dissipative-structure result underwhelms.

### 2. Staged code milestones with gate tests (verification-first)
Each stage has a **gate**: a test that must pass at target tolerance before you proceed.

- **Stage 0 — Non-relativistic gyration (largely done).** Gate: speed conserved to machine precision (~1e-14 ✓); Larmor radius error scaling **second order** in Δt. Add: an actual convergence study (see §5). Target: fitted observed order p = 2.0 ± 0.1 over ≥4 halvings of Δt.
- **Stage 1 — Relativistic pusher (u = γv).** Rewrite the push to advance **momentum** u = γv, not velocity. Implement relativistic Boris, Vay (2008), Higuera & Cary — A. V. Higuera & J. R. Cary, *Structure-preserving second-order integration of relativistic charged particle trajectories in electromagnetic fields*, Phys. Plasmas 24(5), 052104 (2017), DOI 10.1063/1.4979989 — with consistent γ update. Gate A: reproduce the PlasmaPy `BorisIntegrator` doctest (x_t1 = [0.04993754, −0.00249844, 0], v_t1 = [4.9937539, −0.24984385, 0] for B=[0,0,5], v0=[5,0,0], q=m=1, Δt=0.01) to ≤1e-8. Gate B: reproduce the Ripperda **force-free E×B** test (E=−v×B): Boris develops the known spurious drift while Vay/HC stay on the analytic trajectory — this is the Vay (2008) Lorentz-invariance defect. Target: HC and Vay hold γ constant to ≤1e-10 over 1e5 steps; Boris shows the predicted Δt-dependent error.
- **Stage 2 — Drifts & adiabatic invariant with correct diagnostics.** Fix the μ-aliasing trap (see §4f). Gate: grad-B and E×B drift errors ≤1e-3 measured on the *guiding-center* position; μ conserved consistent with adiabatic scaling (∝ powers of r_L/L) across ≥3 values of the adiabaticity parameter.
- **Stage 3 — Genuine analytic tokamak field.** Replace the toy grad-B field (nonzero curl → spurious current) with a divergence-free equilibrium: circular concentric flux surfaces first, then a Solov'ev / Cerfon–Freidberg analytic Grad–Shafranov solution (Cerfon & Freidberg, *"One size fits all" analytic solutions to the Grad–Shafranov equation*, Phys. Plasmas 17, 032502, 2010, DOI 10.1063/1.3328818; MATLAB reference code at math.nyu.edu/~cerfon/codes.html). Gate: div B = 0 to machine precision analytically; passing/trapped (banana) orbit topology correct; **canonical toroidal momentum p_φ = mRu_φ − qψ conserved** to a bounded level in axisymmetry (target: bounded oscillation, no secular drift, over 1e6–1e7 steps).
- **Stage 4 — Relativistic dynamics + parallel E + synchrotron RR.** Add the LL radiation-reaction force via operator splitting (Tamburini et al. 2010). Gate: in a uniform B with no E, a single electron's energy should decay at the analytic synchrotron rate; the **measured phase-space contraction rate** should match Hazeltine–Mahajan (2004). **This is the paper's central figure.**
- **Stage 5 (optional) — Monte-Carlo pitch-angle/collision operator.** Boozer & Kuo-Petravic (Phys. Fluids 24, 851, 1981) or the Eriksson/Helander RE model (Helander, Eriksson & Andersson, *Suppression of runaway electron avalanches by radial diffusion*, Phys. Plasmas 7(10), 4106–4111, 2000, DOI 10.1063/1.1289892), as used in KORC. Gate: relaxation to a known equilibrium distribution; verify the **weak** convergence order of the SDE solver (Euler–Maruyama O(Δt) weak / O(√Δt) strong; Milstein O(Δt) strong), which is the relevant accuracy measure — not the ODE order.

### 4. The specific technical difficulties, with references and mitigations

**(a) Relativistic formulation.** Advance u = γv. The naive relativistic Boris mishandles near-complete E/B cancellation: Vay (J. Comput. Phys., 2008) showed Boris supports the exact solution only when fields vanish and otherwise introduces a spurious, Δt-dependent force that breaks Lorentz invariance in crossed fields. Higuera & Cary (2017) fixed the E×B accuracy *and* restored phase-space volume preservation — verbatim from their abstract, "only the integrator introduced here both preserves volume and gives the correct E×B velocity" (Vay's scheme is not volume-preserving; Boris and HC are). Update γ from the half-step momentum consistently in each scheme. Known residual: even HC cannot capture the nonlinear velocity evolution to machine precision in ultra-relativistic uniform-E cases (Ripperda Fig. 2 shows above-machine-precision position error for all three explicit schemes).

**(b) Timescale separation / stiffness.** Relativistic gyro-period ~10⁻¹¹ s vs RE confinement/acceleration ~10⁻²–1 s → 10¹¹–10¹³ full-orbit steps for a complete trajectory. This is impossible on a laptop in float64 (a single 10⁸-step trajectory array is ~2.4 GB). Defensible strategies, in order of preference for this project: (i) restrict full-orbit runs to **short physical windows** where structure, not machine physics, dominates — legitimate for a *methods* paper (Angle C); (ii) use guiding-center for the long timescale and full-orbit only for diagnostic windows (as KORC/RGC do); (iii) use the volume-preserving secular-dynamics approach of Zhang, Liu, Qin, Wang, He & Sun (Phys. Plasmas 22, 044501, 2015), which explicitly spans "over 10 orders of magnitude" in time for RE; (iv) scaling arguments with artificially enhanced E or reduced timescale separation — permissible only if you show the conclusion is invariant under the scaling. **Long-time conclusions from short windows must be justified** by demonstrating that the diagnosed quantity (e.g., contraction rate, per-step invariant error) is stationary within the window and that the window contains many gyro-periods.

**(c) Dissipative forces and structure preservation (the core).** Use LL, not Lorentz–Abraham–Dirac: LAD has third-order time derivatives and admits unphysical pre-accelerating/runaway (self-accelerating) solutions; LL is the reduction obtained by treating RR perturbatively and is standard (Tamburini et al. 2010). **The key conceptual point for the paper:** once RR is on, phase-space volume is *physically* not conserved — RR contracts it (Hazeltine & Mahajan 2004 derive a fully relativistic kinetic equation and compute the contraction rate; Lehmann & Spatschek 2012 and Gong et al., Matter Radiat. Extremes 1, 308, 2016 show attractors form). Therefore "volume preservation" is the wrong success metric. The right diagnostics are: (i) does the scheme reproduce the *analytic* contraction rate; (ii) does it add spurious numerical contraction/expansion on top; (iii) for the conservative sub-step, is volume still preserved. The aspirational comparison target is the metriplectic/GENERIC family (Morrison 2017; Kraus & Hirvijoki 2017; Hirvijoki, Kraus & Burby 2018) which is designed to respect both the Hamiltonian and the dissipative (entropy-producing) structure — you likely will not implement a full metriplectic integrator, but you should benchmark the standard operator-split scheme against its principles and state clearly what is lost.

**(d) Collision operators (optional Stage 5).** Relativistic Fokker–Planck / Beliaev–Budker for fast electrons; Monte-Carlo via Boozer & Kuo-Petravic (Phys. Fluids 24, 851, 1981); the Helander/Eriksson/Andersson RE model (Phys. Plasmas 7, 4106, 2000) as used in KORC. Treat the Langevin equation as an SDE: the relevant accuracy measure is the **weak convergence order** (for distribution moments), not the ODE order — as emphasized by Dimits, Cohen, Caflisch, Rosin & Ricketson (*Higher-order time integration of Coulomb collisions*), who show Euler–Maruyama is weak-order 1 / strong-order ½ while Milstein achieves strong-order 1 provided the "area-integral" terms are retained. Verify by relaxation to a known Maxwell–Jüttner equilibrium, an H-theorem check, and convergence with particle number N (statistical error ∝ 1/√N).

**(e) Magnetic geometry.** Your toy grad-B field has nonzero curl, implying a spurious current density — not an equilibrium. Move to a Solov'ev / Cerfon–Freidberg analytic Grad–Shafranov solution (Cerfon & Freidberg 2010). Ensure div B = 0 *exactly* (construct B from a flux function / vector potential, not component-wise). p_φ = mRu_φ − qψ (get the relativistic form with u = γv and sign conventions right) is conserved only under exact axisymmetry, so it doubles as a symmetry-breaking diagnostic once ripple or perturbation is added.

**(f) The relativistic magnetic moment.** Standard first-order μ is not conserved for REs; the C. Liu et al. (2018) corrected μ includes a second-order curvature term (of the same order as the leading term when p∥ ≫ p⊥) and is well conserved **up to ~80 MeV** (verbatim: the theory is "invalid for runaway electron with extremely high energy (>80MeV)... a full-orbit particle simulation is required"). **Practical trap:** sampling μ once per gyro-orbit (as the mirror test does) aliases the gyro-phase and can manufacture apparent non-conservation or hide it. Mitigation: gyro-average μ over each period, or sample at fixed gyro-phase, or use the analytic gyro-averaging in the corrected-μ expression.

**(g) Diagnostic pitfalls.** (i) Measure drifts on the extracted guiding center x_gc = x + m(v×B)/(qB²), not on x(t) — you already found the ~30% error from measuring displacement smaller than one Larmor radius. (ii) For leapfrog, reconstruct a time-centred velocity and use the time-centred kinetic energy 0.5·m·v_{n−1/2}·v_{n+1/2} to avoid the chord-shortening artefact. (iii) Beware aliasing at once-per-gyro-period sampling. (iv) Crucially, **separate numerical error from physical non-conservation**: run a convergence study — genuine physical non-conservation of an adiabatic invariant is Δt-independent as Δt→0, whereas numerical error vanishes with the scheme's order. This distinction is the backbone of the whole project.

**(h) Performance & memory.** Do **on-the-fly reduction**: accumulate invariant errors, extrema, and contraction rates in scalars/running statistics rather than storing full trajectories; use strided/checkpointed output; vectorize over an ensemble axis with NumPy and, if needed, compile the kernel with Numba; use float64 throughout (conservation studies are meaningless in float32). Initialize leapfrog carefully — your −qΔt/(4m) half-step back-up is O(Δt³) inconsistent and injects an offset at the 1e-15 level; use a consistent Taylor initialization and note the offset in the paper.

### 5. Verification & Validation methodology (what referees at CPC/PoP/JCP/EJP expect)
- **Verification vs validation** (Roache 1998; Oberkampf & Roy, *Verification and Validation in Scientific Computing*, CUP 2010; Oberkampf & Trucano, Prog. Aerospace Sci. 38, 209, 2002): verification = "are we solving the equations right" (math); validation = "are we solving the right equations" (vs experiment/physics). For a test-particle code, most of your burden is **verification**.
- **Code verification vs solution verification** (Roy, J. Comput. Phys. 205, 131, 2005): code verification establishes the implementation's order of accuracy; solution verification estimates the error in a particular run.
- **Method of Manufactured Solutions** (Salari & Knupp, SAND2000-1444; Roache, J. Fluids Eng. 124, 4, 2002): fabricate an exact orbit by adding a source term, then confirm the scheme recovers it at design order. Use it on the pusher.
- **Order-of-accuracy verification:** run ≥4–5 systematic Δt halvings; fit log(error) vs log(Δt) by least squares; report the **observed order p** with its fit uncertainty and confirm it matches the theoretical 2 (Boris/Vay/HC). A scheme that fails to hit design order signals a bug.
- **Richardson extrapolation & Grid Convergence Index** (Roache): use to estimate the error of the finest run and report a GCI as an error bar.
- **Fair (equal-cost) integrator benchmark:** compare at equal wall-clock / equal force-evaluations, not equal Δt. RK4 uses 4 force evaluations per step, so a fair comparison gives RK4 ¼ the steps of Boris. This is exactly the failing in your current energy test. Ripperda et al. (2018) and Yasir & Saxena (2024) show how to present per-step cost (operation counts; Boris cheapest, Vay ~91 ops/step) alongside accuracy.
- **Demonstrating a BOUNDED energy error:** your current test cannot claim boundedness because max error = final error (no plateau shown). To demonstrate boundedness you must run *long enough to see the error oscillate and plateau* — for Boris this is guaranteed by volume preservation and KAM-type arguments (Qin et al. 2013: the global bound on energy error typically associated with symplectic algorithms still holds for Boris because it is volume-preserving; Hairer & Lubich, BIT 58, 969, 2018, show the energy error is bounded for exponentially long times when the invariant tori survive, and grows linearly otherwise). Diagnostic: plot max-so-far error and running mean over ≥10⁶–10⁷ gyro-periods; show the envelope saturates rather than growing linearly. Contrast with RK4's secular linear growth.
- **Reproducibility (CPC-specific):** CPC requires the *program itself* be submitted under an approved open-source licence (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD 3-clause/2-clause, GPLv3/v2, LGPL, MPL-2.0, CeCILL, etc.) and archived in the CPC Program Library on Mendeley Data; the paper must include a structured Program Summary (Nature of problem / Solution method / Restrictions and unusual features). General best practice: pin the environment (requirements.txt / conda lockfile), fix and report RNG seeds, archive code+data with a Zenodo DOI, and provide a pytest/property-based test suite (move the assertions out of the demo functions).

- **PlasmaPy** (`BorisIntegrator` / `RelativisticBorisIntegrator`): match the published doctest values exactly (≤1e-8). Also reproduce its E×B and grad-B example notebooks. Success = agreement to round-off in the single-step push.
- **Ripperda et al. 2018 test suite:** gyration, E×B (including force-free), grad-B drift, relativistic gyration. Success = your Boris/Vay/HC curves overlay theirs; Boris shows the spurious force-free drift, Vay/HC do not.
- **KORC** (github.com/ORNL-Fusion/KORC): use its analytic circular field (B₀=2.1 T, R₀=1.5 m, q₀=1, q_edge=3, r_edge=0.5 m) as a common testbed; compare confined-fraction vs energy and synchrotron power. (Note KORC needs Fortran/MPI/HDF5 — heavy for a laptop; use it as a reference for *published figures* rather than rerunning.)
- **DREAM** (Hoppe, Embreus & Fulop, *DREAM: A fluid-kinetic framework for tokamak disruption runaway electron simulations*, Comput. Phys. Commun. 268, 108098, 2021, DOI 10.1016/j.cpc.2021.108098): distribution-function-level benchmark for the collision/avalanche physics if you reach Stage 5. Open-source on GitHub (chalmersplasmatheory/DREAM).
- **SOFT/SOFT2** (Hoppe, Embreus, Tinguely, Granetz, Stahl & Fulop, *SOFT: a synthetic synchrotron diagnostic for runaway electrons*, Nucl. Fusion 58, 026032, 2018): synchrotron-image benchmark if you add a synthetic diagnostic.
- **Solov'ev orbit benchmarks** (Cerfon & Freidberg analytic equilibria; Youjun Hu's orbit notes): confirm passing/trapped orbit topology and p_φ conservation.
For each: state the compared quantity and the agreement threshold *before* running, so success is falsifiable.

### 7. Critical review of the six existing tests
1. **Uniform-B gyration, Larmor error 4.9e-4, speed to 1.4e-15.** The speed conservation is the exact-rotation property of Boris and is correct. The 4.9e-4 radius error at ω_c·Δt ≈ 0.063: for a second-order scheme the leading phase/geometry error scales as (ω_cΔt)²/12 ≈ 3.3e-4 — same order as observed, consistent with second order. **Fix:** turn this into a convergence plot and fit p ≈ 2. As reported it demonstrates correctness at one Δt, not order.
2. **Long-time energy, Boris 4.8% bounded vs RK4 93% loss at ω_cΔt=0.314 over 10⁴ orbits.** *Unfair* (equal Δt) and *does not prove boundedness* (max = final error). **Fix:** equal-cost comparison; run 10⁶–10⁷ orbits; plot the error envelope to show saturation for Boris and linear growth for RK4.
3. **E×B drift, 3.2e-4.** Fine as a point check; fold into a convergence study.
4. **grad-B drift, 1.4e-4 after GC extraction (30% on raw x).** Correct and an important lesson — keep the GC-extraction explanation in the paper; it is pedagogically valuable. Note the field's nonzero curl invalidates it as an equilibrium; move to Solov'ev.
5. **Mirror, μ to 3e-3 at r_L/L=1.1e-2, |v| to 3e-14.** Adiabatic theory: the leading μ variation is exponentially small in the adiabaticity parameter ε=r_L/L in the ideal limit, but the *measured* per-bounce error typically scales polynomially (∝ε or ε²) depending on sampling. 3e-3 at ε≈1.1e-2 is ~0.27·ε — plausible but **you must show the scaling across several ε** to claim it is adiabatic rather than numerical, and you must fix the once-per-orbit μ sampling (aliasing risk).
6. **Gyro-phase error tabulation, ω_eff = ω_c(1−(ω_cΔt)²/12), 191 orbits per radian at ω_cΔt=0.1.** Correct and the (ω_cΔt)²/12 coefficient is the standard Boris phase-lag — this is your best-verified result and a good verification anchor.

**Overall:** the results are *satisfying as a first pedagogical pass* and are internally correct, but they demonstrate correctness at single operating points rather than convergence, boundedness, or anything relativistic. None is yet publishable as RE physics.

## Caveats
- **Reference-verification flags:** the Higuera–Cary 2017 paper is confirmed as Phys.
